Frogs swallow with their eyeballs. 🐸👀

When a frog gulps, it blinks hard and pulls its eyes down into its head. There's no bone under a frog's eyes, just a sheet of muscle, so the eyeballs press down into the mouth and help push the food toward the throat.

In a study of northern leopard frogs, X-ray video showed the eyes pushing crickets down. When the eye-pulling muscles were switched off, the frogs could still swallow, but needed 4.0 gulps per cricket instead of 2.3, about 74% more.

Bonus: a frog's tongue is about 10 times softer than ours, and its spit goes runny when it hits a bug, then thick and sticky to hold on.

Source: Levine, Monroy & Brainerd (2004), Contribution of eye retraction to swallowing performance in the northern leopard frog, https://sicb.org/?p=34751 · Noel et al. (2017), J R Soc Interface 14:20160764 · Eye-socket anatomy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7671152/
