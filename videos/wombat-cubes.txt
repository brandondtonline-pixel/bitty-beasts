Why do wombats poop cubes? 🟫

A bare-nosed wombat makes 80–100 little cube-shaped droppings a day and leaves them in piles on rocks, logs and other landmarks, probably as signposts for other wombats.

The cubes take shape only in the last 17% of the gut, where they also dry out (from about 81% water to 53%). The gut wall there has patches about twice as thick and four times as stiff as the rest, and in the researchers' 2D model those stiff patches squeeze faster, so corners form.

Each one is about 2.5 cm across and 4 cm long, so technically they're little bricks. The flat sides may help them stay put, but that's still an open question.

Source: Yang et al. 2021, "Intestines of non-uniform stiffness mold the corners of wombat feces", Soft Matter 17, 475–488, https://doi.org/10.1039/D0SM01230K
