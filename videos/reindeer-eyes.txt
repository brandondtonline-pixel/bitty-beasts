Reindeer eyes change color with the seasons. 🦌✨

At the back of a reindeer's eye is a mirror-like layer that helps it see in dim light. In summer it's golden and bounces light straight back out. In the long, dark Arctic winter it turns deep blue, which scatters more light onto the light-sensing cells instead. Scientists think this helps reindeer see in near-darkness, though it makes their vision a little blurrier.

Reindeer can also see ultraviolet light. Snow reflects lots of UV, but lichen (their favourite winter food) and wolf fur absorb it, so in UV they may show up dark against the snow.

Source: Stokkan et al. 2013, Proc R Soc B, via UCL https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2013/oct/reindeers-eyes-change-colour-arctic-seasons · Hogg et al. 2011, J Exp Biol 214:2014, via UCL https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/news-articles/1105/11052502
