Do foxes have a built-in compass? 🦊🧭

Red foxes hunt mice they can't see by listening for them under the snow, then leaping high and diving in head-first. Scientists in the Czech Republic watched 84 wild foxes make 592 of these jumps and found they mostly pounced facing roughly north-east. Facing that way, 73% of pounces caught something; facing the opposite way 60%; any other direction just 18%.

One idea (still unproven) is that foxes use Earth's magnetic field like a range finder: when the mouse's rustling reaches their ears at the same angle as the field dips into the ground, it's time to jump. And their pointy snouts are shaped for diving into snow at 2–4 metres a second.

Source: Červený et al. 2011, Biology Letters 7:355, via National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/foxes-use-the-earths-magnetic-field-as-a-targeting-system · Snow-diving study, https://www.sci.news/biology/mousing-foxes-12897.html
